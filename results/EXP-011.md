# EXP-011 — Does visual diversity select better OCR training pages?

**Completed seed-824 k-center pilot · Qwen3-VL-4B · READ2016 official validation.** Entropy, confidence, and repeat-seed comparisons in EXP-011 are still running or pending.

We tested whether choosing visually different pages helps whole-page transcription more than choosing pages at random. Both paths used the **same untouched Qwen model**, the same first 64 randomly selected TRAIN pages and saved adapter, and the same 64-new-pages-per-round budget. After that shared checkpoint, k-center selected the 64 pages farthest from the labelled set in frozen Qwen visual-feature space, updated coverage after each choice, then repeated once more. Each 128- and 192-page fit restarted from the pinned base model with the cumulative selected true transcripts; this simulates human annotation without a labelling UI. Training settings, decoding, and scoring matched the [random control](EXP-010.md).

We evaluated each saved checkpoint on the same 42 official READ2016 validation pages. **CER** counts character edits per reference character; **WER** counts word edits per reference word. Lower is better. An output cut off by the fixed token limit counts as an empty prediction; the final column shows these cases. The base and 64-page rows are shared checkpoints, so no selection comparison exists at those budgets.

| Labelled TRAIN pages | Random CER ↓ | K-center CER ↓ | Random WER ↓ | K-center WER ↓ | Truncated random / k-center |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 0.7902 | 0.7902 | 1.0019 | 1.0019 | 10 / 10 |
| 64 | 0.5025 | 0.5025 | 0.8643 | 0.8643 | 3 / 3 |
| 128 | 0.2946 | 0.3113 | 0.6841 | 0.6976 | 1 / 1 |
| 192 | 0.2112 | 0.1994 | 0.5589 | 0.5574 | 0 / 0 |

K-center covered the frozen visual-feature space more evenly than random, but **the OCR result is mixed**. It was worse at 128 pages: CER increased by 0.0167. At 192 pages, CER decreased by 0.0118 and WER was almost unchanged. This one seed does not establish that k-center generally improves active learning. Visual difference may not identify the pages whose transcripts teach the model most, and a score based on image features does not measure transcription errors directly.

This is engineering evidence on **official validation**, not a document-independent final TEST result. Document grouping and possible pretraining overlap are unknown. Repeat-seed controls and the entropy/confidence strategies are needed before assessing a reliable selection advantage. Equal page counts also do not measure human annotation time.
