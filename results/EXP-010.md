# EXP-010 — Can Qwen learn whole-page historical transcription from selected pages?

**Completed 20 September 2026 · READ2016 · Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct**

## Why we ran it

Earlier Qwen experiments tried to produce both line boxes and text, but full-page joint outputs were unreliable. This experiment gives Qwen the same **page-text task and 64-page-per-round training schedule** used by LightOnOCR in [EXP-009](EXP-009.md). It asks whether Qwen improves as existing true transcripts from randomly acquired training pages are revealed. The random schedule is a baseline for future active-learning strategies, not itself evidence that active learning beats random selection.

## What happened

The untouched Qwen model transcribed pages first. Next, 64 fresh READ2016 TRAIN pages were selected each round; the original source transcripts simulated annotation. For the three separate fits the model restarted from the same pinned base and trained on cumulative sets of 64, 128 and 192 pages. It stopped at round three, not when validation scores improved. Both models' page sets and numeric training/inference settings are documented together in the [shared recipe](../experiments/recipes/read2016-page-text.json). Neither model detected or scored line boxes.

The official READ2016 split has 350 TRAIN and 50 VAL pages. We checked the recipe on eight VAL pages, then evaluated the **same saved Qwen checkpoints** on the other 42 VAL pages. No final TEST set was used. True VAL text stayed in the local evaluator, outside training and prediction requests.

**CER** is total character edits divided by true characters; **WER** is word edits divided by true words. Lower is better; extra text can make either score greater than 1. Predictions stopped by the fixed 1,536-token cap count as empty, and are shown as failed/truncated pages.

| TRAIN pages labelled | Qwen VAL42 CER ↓ | Qwen VAL42 WER ↓ | Truncated / 42 | LightOnOCR EXP-009 VAL42 CER ↓ |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 0.790 | 1.002 | 10/42 | 0.781 |
| 64 | 0.502 | 0.864 | 3/42 | 0.568 |
| 128 | 0.295 | 0.684 | 1/42 | 0.362 |
| 192 | 0.211 | 0.559 | 0/42 | 0.216 |

On the separate eight-page recipe slice, Qwen CER was **0.697 → 0.440 → 0.281 → 0.196** for 0/64/128/192 pages. The 42-page Qwen CER changed by **73.3% relative to its untouched baseline**. The baseline ran out of output tokens on 10 of 42 pages, making its apparent starting error especially sensitive to the fixed output cap. The per-round truncation counts above matter when interpreting improvement.

## What this means for the project

The table compares two models on the **same official pages and annotation budgets**, using the same page-text evaluator and numeric training settings. Their architectures, tokenizers, image/chat processors and prompts differ; EXP-009's 42-page LightOnOCR scores came from a separate pilot whose adapters and hardware differ from its eight-page runner. Treat the figures as an engineering model comparison, not a controlled claim about architecture or compute. A model-informed selection strategy at identical page budgets, repeat seeds and an independently justified document-grouped test are still needed to measure active-learning benefit or generalization.

The [technical EXP-010 record](../experiments/EXP-010.md) provides the exact model/data revisions, shared recipe, run links, adapter hashes, verification and locations of saved trained adapters and raw predictions. The adapters are held in the Modal Volume and mirrored under ignored `.local/models/` in the codebase; large weights and private labels are deliberately excluded from Git.
